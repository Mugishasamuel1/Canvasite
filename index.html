01 — Identity

One person.
Many languages.

"I am because we are."

I am a Senior 6 student at Agahozo-Shalom Youth Village in Rwanda — a place that transforms lives. My work spans the stage and the screen, the debate floor and the farmland, the music studio and the mathematics classroom. I do not compartmentalize these worlds. They are all expressions of the same belief: that creativity and rigor, art and logic, community and ambition are not opposites — they are one.

I build technology to solve real problems for farmers. I perform original music at corporate events for thousands. I chair diplomatic committees. I wash dishes. Every one of these is, to me, equally sacred.

02 — The Arc

A story told
chronologically.

2023 · Foundation
First step onto the GHAMUN floor

As a first-year delegate at Green Hills Academy MUN, I immersed myself in international law, learning to listen before I spoke. This year was about absorbing — the grammar of diplomacy, the patience of negotiation, the art of building coalitions in a room of strangers. I left with one thing: the hunger to come back better.

Leadership · Diplomacy
2023 · Recognition
Pi Day Mathematics Award

Presented by a representative from the University of Rwanda and my School Principal, this award marked something deeper than an academic win. It confirmed that the analytical muscle I train every day in mathematics is the same muscle I use when composing a melody — both are pattern recognition at their core.

Mathematics · Excellence
2024 · Breakthrough
Ecobank End-of-Year Staff Performance

A corporate hall. A massive audience. Davido's "Aye" — chosen deliberately for its crowd energy. When the room erupted, something shifted in me. This was not a school stage. This was the industry. And I was ready. This performance remains the single most formative artistic moment of my journey.

Music · Stage Presence
2024 · Research
GHAMUN — Three winning resolutions

At Green Hills Academy MUN for the second time, I returned not as a learner but as a strategist. I drafted three winning resolutions in UNDP and C40, mastering data-driven argument construction and the precision of persuasive writing under parliamentary pressure.

Research · Writing
2024 · Creation
EZA for Farmers — Platform Launched

I built EZA not because it was assigned, but because I looked around and saw a gap. Rwandan farmers needed modern agricultural guidance. I built them a platform — PHP, MySQL, JavaScript — that delivers training virtually and one-on-one. This is technology with a face and a purpose.

Innovation · Social Impact
2025 · Leadership
ASYVMUN Chair — leading the room

Chairing my own MUN conference was the transition from advocate to architect. I moderated heated debates, mentored first-time delegates, and held the room's decorum — not with authority, but with earned trust. I understood that a great chair, like a great performer, must make the room feel safe enough to be brilliant.

Leadership · Mentorship
2025 · Voice
Original Single: "Dollars"

My debut single — a fusion of Afro-pop rhythms and personal narrative. "Dollars" is not about money. It is about the hours you put in when no one is watching. It is about wanting something so badly you can taste it. Writing it forced me to define my sound, my story, and my artistic identity.

Songwriting · Production
03 — The Stage

Music is not
what I do. It is
how I think.

01

Agahozo & Friends Concert

Performing at the concert of my own village was personal in the truest sense. Every brother and sister in that crowd had shared the same corridors, the same meals, the same dreams deferred and reclaimed. The stage that night was not a platform — it was a promise. Music as a returning of everything the community had given me.

02

Ecobank Corporate Showcase

The largest audience I had ever faced. A room of corporate professionals expecting entertainment — and I gave them something to feel. Choosing "Aye" was a calculated risk that paid off in a standing moment. I learned that a performer's greatest skill is reading a room before the first note lands.

03

Iwacu Fashion Show

Music at a fashion show must do something specific: it must inhabit the space between the garments and the gaze of the audience. I performed as the sonic architecture of an evening built on Rwandan identity and design. It sharpened my understanding of music as atmosphere, not just entertainment.

04

Melodies in Motion

A student-organized concert — and the most professionally executed show I had been part of at the time. Transitioning from intimate acoustic passages to high-energy peaks, I found my natural range as a live performer: versatile, dynamic, and always in conversation with the crowd.

05

ASYV Village Time — Weekly

Every Friday, students at Agahozo-Shalom showcase their talents. I used this not as a performance slot but as a laboratory — testing new material, refining stage movement, learning to hold attention in a space where the audience knows every performer personally. The most demanding crowd of all.

06

"Dollars" — Original Single

Writing "Dollars" was the hardest and most necessary thing I have done musically. Every lyric had to be true. Every beat had to serve the narrative. The result is a song about ambition stripped of its glamour — the unglamorous daily work of becoming who you intend to be.

04 — The Floor

Diplomacy taught me
what the stage could not.

23
The Foundation

Immersed in international law. Learning that listening is itself a form of power. First exposure to parliamentary procedure and the architecture of a winning resolution.

24
The Researcher

Three winning resolutions in UNDP and C40. Mastered data-driven argument construction. Attended GHAMUN twice. Discovered that persuasion is not rhetoric — it is precision in the service of empathy.

25
The Leader

Transitioned to Chair at ASYVMUN. Prepared and organized the conference itself. Chaired committees: UNEP, SOCHUM, UNICEF. Learned that the best leaders speak least and enable most.

Three consecutive years at Green Hills Academy MUN. Each year a different version of me walked onto that floor — and each year I left having outgrown the version who arrived. This is what I look for in every discipline I pursue: not completion, but a trajectory.

Chairing ASYVMUN was the culmination — not of a committee, but of a way of leading. Parliamentary procedure demands that you hold two things at once: the structure of the debate and the humanity of the debaters. I tried to hold both.
